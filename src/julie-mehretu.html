Julie Mehretu

Abstraction as a way to investigate how architecture, history, movement, and collective experience shape the spaces we inhabit.

Overview

Julie Mehretu (born 1970 in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia) developed a painting practice that draws on maps, architectural plans, gestural marks, and historical imagery. Rather than depicting a single recognizable place, her layered compositions suggest multiple systems and perspectives operating simultaneously (Whitney Museum of American Art, n.d.; Los Angeles County Museum of Art, n.d.).

Her work has evolved from diagrammatic, spatially complex compositions toward increasingly gestural paintings with saturated color and blurred or obscured source imagery. The 2021 Whitney exhibition, co-organized with LACMA, traced that evolution through paintings and works on paper spanning more than two decades (Whitney Museum of American Art, 2021; Los Angeles County Museum of Art, n.d.).

Why the work matters

Mehretu demonstrates how abstraction can engage with social and political histories without functioning as literal illustration. Architectural references and marks can be read as competing structures of organization and movement. This is an interpretive synthesis based on the exhibition accounts and artist documentation cited here, rather than a direct statement from the artist (Whitney Museum of American Art, 2021; High Museum of Art, 2020).

Research questions

  • How does an abstract painting communicate histories without representing specific events?
  • What changes when an architectural drawing becomes an expressive pictorial structure?
  • How do scale and movement alter a viewer's reading of the painted surface?
