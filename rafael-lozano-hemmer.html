Participation, surveillance, public space, and the relationship between bodies and technological systems.

Rafael Lozano-Hemmer (born 1967 in Mexico City) creates installations that enlist sensors, projections, light, and computational systems to respond to participants. His work often uses technological systems to make public interaction visible (North Carolina Museum of Art, n.d.).

Why this artist next? Unlike several institutional sources for Julie Mehretu, Lozano-Hemmer's own artwork image pages explicitly identify a Creative Commons Attribution–NonCommercial–ShareAlike 4.0 International license. This is a promising path to direct image display on a noncommercial educational site, but the license scope and photographer credit must be checked for each individual reproduction before copying or embedding.

Interpretive framework

Lozano-Hemmer's practice provides a useful comparison with artists who work primarily in static media: the viewer may become an active participant whose movements or presence help constitute the work. His installations raise questions about agency, observation, and technological mediation (North Carolina Museum of Art, n.d.).
